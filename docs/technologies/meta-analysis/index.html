SARS-CoV-2 airway infection results in the development of somatosensory abnormalities in a hamster model

Intranasal SARS-CoV-2 infection of golden hamsters deposits viral RNA without infectious virus in dorsal root ganglia and spinal cord, producing a milder but longer-lasting mechanical hypersensitivity than influenza A virus and a neuropathic dorsal root ganglion transcriptome at 31 days, from which ILF3 emerges as an analgesic target validated in mouse pain models.
